User Journey

Meeting preparation for amasol and klaricore sales reps
The one rule: the rep's word is final Whatever the rep says overrides everything else: web research, Apollo, Treg and anything saved from earlier. If research disagrees with the rep, the brief uses the rep's version. The difference is only shown to the rep as a note ("Apollo lists her as Deputy CIO, you said CIO; using CIO"), never used to change what the rep said.

1

Create the customer

  • The rep creates a new customer by entering the company name (the website is optional).
  • The system finds the company and shows what it found: legal name, headquarters, and the group it belongs to (e.g. "Siemens AG, Munich, not Siemens Healthineers?").
  • The rep confirms or corrects it. Their choice is final.
  • If the customer already exists, the rep goes into that customer, and everything known about it is already there.
2

Rep puts in everything they have

The rep enters what they know: typed, pasted (emails, invites, notes) or as files. The system picks out the standard details:

Standard detailNeeded?Example
Company nameRequiredMüller Logistik GmbH
Who is attendingRequiredMaria Huber, Tom Berger, Anna Klein
Their designationsRequiredCIO · Head of IT Operations · unknown
Agenda / purpose of the meetingRequiredFirst meeting to discuss their monitoring setup
What a good outcome looks likeRequiredAgree a workshop with their operations team
Meeting dateRequired12 March, on site
Who from our side attendsOptionalRep + solution architect
Anything already knownOptionalPast emails, call notes, an RFP, a referral

Everything the rep puts in is tagged REP and is final.

3

Gap check: fill in what is missing

The system compares what the rep gave against the standard details, does one full check, and shows one list of all gaps. Each gap is answered by the rep or handed on to research:

Kind of gapWhat happensExample
Only the rep can know
agenda, good outcome, meeting date, who attends		 Asked from the rep. If the rep skips it, the brief says it is missing. "What would a good outcome be for this meeting?"
The rep might know
designations, earlier contact, current tools		 Asked from the rep with a "I don't know" answer. "I don't know" moves it to the research list. "What is Anna Klein's role?" → I don't know → research
Research questions
things the rep wouldn't know		 Go straight to the research list, together with the rep's "I don't know" answers. "Which monitoring tools do they use today?"
  • The rep can answer all gaps at once, answer some, or start research anyway.
  • The research list is shown to the rep before research starts, so they know what will be looked for.
4

Check the customer record

  • Before searching, the system loads everything already known about this customer: facts from earlier research, the rep's notes from past meetings, people met before, and questions still open.
  • Anything already known is not searched again, unless it is old enough to check again. Research focuses on what is new since last time. Earlier statements from reps stay final.
5

Research

Research runs on three sources at the same time, and each topic and each attendee is researched separately:

SourceWhat it gives
WebCompany websites, annual reports, investor material, press releases, regulators and business registers, conference pages and speaker lists, tender portals, news and trade press, job postings, vendor case studies, engineering blogs, public code, interviews
ApolloCompany data (size, revenue, industry, tech stack, headcount trends, job postings) and people data (title, seniority, work history, LinkedIn profile)
TregAccess to many data providers in one place: people and company enrichment, funding, buying signals, and a second source to check Apollo against

Research method: which sources count most

#SourceUsed for
1The company itself (website, annual reports, investor material, press releases) and regulatorsFacts
2Reputable press, analysts and trade pressFacts
3Job postings (careers pages, job boards), professional profiles, and data providers like Apollo and TregFacts and signals
4Blogs, forums and social postsOnly to back up other sources

When sources disagree, the higher one wins. Above all of them: the rep's word.

  • Every finding is kept with its source and date, so the rep can open it.
  • Every item on the research list from step 3 is either found, partly found, or marked as not found.
  • The rep sees progress while research runs (e.g. "Company ✓ · Tech stack … · Attendee 2 of 3 …").
6

Make sense of it

  • Pick out what matters for what we sell: their current tools, recent changes and likely problems.
  • Separate confirmed from guess. Every statement in the brief carries one tag:
TagMeans
REPThe rep said it. Final; overrides everything.
CONFIRMEDBacked by a strong source (rank 1–2), or by two independent sources.
LIKELYA guess from evidence, shown with its reason ("their job ads ask for X and Y").
UNKNOWNImportant, but neither the rep nor research could answer it.
  • Apply the rep's word. Where research disagrees with the rep, the rep's version is used and the difference is noted for the rep only.
  • Turn guesses and gaps into questions (see "What happens to a gap" below).

7

The meeting brief

Short enough to read in a few minutes before the meeting. Every line carries its tag and, where it came from research, its source.

Questions to ask

ListWhat goes in itExample

B. Curated questions

8

The meeting

  • The rep uses the brief before and during the meeting (it reads well on a phone).
  • The rep asks the questions and can tick each one off and jot a short answer as they go.
9

Rep notes what they learned

  • After the meeting, the rep notes what they learned, in any form: typed notes, a pasted email, the ticked questions.
  • The system turns this into facts for the customer record. The rep sees what will be saved and confirms it.
  • Everything the rep says is saved as REP and is final: answered questions are closed, guesses are confirmed or dropped, and wrong research facts are replaced.
  • Agreed next steps are saved, ready for the next meeting.
↺ Next time: the rep goes into the same customer, enters the new meeting's details, and the brief starts from everything already known; research looks only for what is new.
Important customers: the rep is told when something big changes (new leaders, takeovers, outages, big IT tenders), even with no meeting booked.
Help our reps prepare for any customer meeting in minutes, and remember everything we learn about each customer so every meeting starts smarter than the last.
